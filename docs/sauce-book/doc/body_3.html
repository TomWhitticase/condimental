06 · Chilli Mayo — DATA

IMG:card_06_chilli_mayo

The point of it. The cross-product. Two tiers you have already paid for, meeting at one mixer for the price of a data edit. Nothing is bought and nothing is built.

In a kitchen

Four parts mayonnaise to one part hot sauce, folded in with a spatula rather than whisked — whisking shears the emulsion and you get a thin pink liquid instead of a sauce. A squeeze of lime and a pinch of smoked paprika if you want the chipotle version.

In the game

Recipe(id: "chilli_mayo", fluid_inputs: [("mayonnaise", 2), ("hot_sauce", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("chilli_mayo", 1)],
       machine: "mixer", time_ticks: 80),
Recipe(id: "bottled_chilli_mayo", fluid_inputs: [("chilli_mayo", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_chilli_mayo", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_chilli_mayo", inputs: [("bottled_chilli_mayo", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_chilli_mayo", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Two taps — one off the mayonnaise run, one off the hot sauce run — into a mixer (80t), then bottler, packer, dock. Three machines and a dock, all of kinds you own, and the two taps are the entire build.

Machines

None. Not one.

Why it is fun

Because unlocking hot sauce quietly unlocked this too, and the player finds out by reading the contract board. It is the whole argument for a sauce tree rather than a sauce list: late capital pays off across every branch at once, and the most expensive thing in the book (the fermenter bank) suddenly has a second customer. It also raises the stakes on the mayo run for a third time — mayonnaise, aioli and chilli mayo all drink from it — which is the point at which a player stops extending the factory and starts re-planning it.

The money

77 per box, all of it sauce you made. Contracts pay 210–235 — the biggest line in the book until tartare turns up at 08. Gate: 95 lifetime deliveries, which is comfortably past both parents; there is no gate on the recipe itself, because in practice the tree is the gate — without a mayonnaise run and a hot sauce run there is nothing to tap.

07 · House Reserve Steak Sauce — DATA

IMG:card_07_house_reserve

The point of it. Tier 7, and not one new machine. It is gated by your floor plan: three pipe runs and a hopper into one 2×2 vat, without crossing.

In a kitchen

Equal parts tomato-based BBQ sauce and wholegrain mustard, a quarter part hot sauce, two tablespoons of molasses, a splash of vinegar. Reduce twenty minutes until it coats a spoon (nappe — it should hold a line drawn through it on the back of the spoon). Bottle hot and wax the cap. It is a steakhouse table sauce: sweet, sour, sharp and hot at once, which is exactly what makes it the top of the tree — it needs one sauce from every branch.

In the game

Recipe(id: "house_reserve", inputs: [("molasses", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("bbq", 1), ("mustard", 1), ("hot_sauce", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("house_reserve", 1)],
       machine: "cooking_vat", time_ticks: 400),
Recipe(id: "bottled_house_reserve", fluid_inputs: [("house_reserve", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_house_reserve", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_house_reserve", inputs: [("bottled_house_reserve", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_house_reserve", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Three taps — BBQ, mustard, hot sauce — plus a hopper of molasses, all into one cooking vat (400t). Pipe out to a bottler (the wax dip is cosmetic), belt, packer, dock.

Three fluid draws at one machine has never shipped. BBQ's cook draws two, and the fluid scope was widened to two for it; the registry holds a list and does not cap it, but the code path has never been exercised at three, so this needs a test before it needs anything else.

Machines

None new. A fifth cooking-vat recipe, a ninth bottler recipe, a ninth packer recipe.

Why it is fun

A 2×2 vat has eight perimeter tiles and the outlet face eats two of them. Three pipe runs and a hopper have to reach the remaining six without crossing each other or the three lines already running through that part of your factory. That is the whole puzzle, and it is a spatial one — the payoff tier of the game is a layout tier, and it costs nothing to build but a rethink. Players will tear down a quarter of the floor to make room for it, and that teardown is the memorable bit.

The money

56 per box — three sauces you chose not to ship, plus molasses. Contracts pay 190–210. Note that it pays less per box than chilli mayo while eating three finished sauces instead of two: the house reserve is a prestige sauce, and what it really costs you is three finished runs diverted away from orders you could have filled. Gate: 150 lifetime deliveries — past every branch it draws from. It releases no ingredient at all: the only thing this tier unlocks is contracts, because everything it consumes you already make.

P · Pickles — DATA, AND NOT A SAUCE

IMG:card_08a_pickles

The point of it. The last two sauces in this book are both pickle sauces, so the pickle line is drawn once, here, and both of them tap it. It is the first thing the factory makes that nobody buys: there is no bottled pickle, no boxed pickle and no pickle contract. It exists to feed two mixers, and that is the entire point of it.

In a kitchen

Small ridged cucumbers, scrubbed, packed upright in a jar with 3 % brine, a splash of vinegar and a head of dill. Left at room temperature they go cloudy in two days and sour in a week — that cloud is the lactic ferment, the same one the hot sauce mash runs, but a quick pickle is days where a chilli mash is weeks. Drain, top and tail, and dice to 3 mm: tartare wants it fine enough to spread, not to crunch.

In the game

Recipe(id: "pickles", inputs: [("cucumber", 3), ("salt", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("vinegar", 1)],
       outputs: [("pickle", 1)],
       machine: "fermenter", time_ticks: 600),
Recipe(id: "diced_pickle", inputs: [("pickle", 1)],
       outputs: [("diced_pickle", 1)], machine: "crusher", time_ticks: 80),

The line

Hoppers of cucumber and salt, and a pipe of vinegar, into a fermenter (600t — twenty seconds, a fifth of the hot sauce mash, because a quick brine pickle is not a months-long ferment and should not cost like one) → belt → crusher (80t, dicing rather than pulping) → belt. That belt is the output. It goes to a mixer, or to two mixers, and never to a bottler.

Machines

None new, and this is the nice part: the fermenter earns a second recipe and the crusher a fifth. If hot sauce has already been built, the bank of fermenters standing on the floor is suddenly dual-use, and the player's most expensive purchase in the book gets a second job without buying anything. Note that pickles and fermented_mash share no input id at all, so the fermenter needs nothing from §7 — it is disjoint under the shipped rule.

Why it is fun

A sub-assembly. Every factory game's moment of no return is the first line you build that has no customer outside the factory, and CONDIMENTAL does not have one yet — every belt today ends at a dock. The pickle line ends at a junction. It also makes a ratio problem out of nothing, and the numbers are pleasingly awkward: a fermenter yields one pickle per 600 ticks, a crusher dices one per 80, and a tartare mixer eats one per 100. So six fermenters and one crusher feed one mixer — the crusher has headroom to spare and the fermenter bank does not, which is the same lesson hot sauce teaches, arriving a second time in a form the player can now do arithmetic about.

The money

9 per unit of diced pickle in ingredients (cucumber 2×3, salt 1, vinegar 2×1), and one crate of cucumbers makes one pail. No contract, ever. The cost lands in the two sauces below.

08 · Tartare — DATA

IMG:card_08_tartare

The point of it. Mayonnaise with things in it. Every sauce shipped or proposed so far is a flat colour; tartare is the first one with visible bits, which makes it the first bottle on the shelf you can identify at play zoom without reading a label.

In a kitchen

Two parts mayonnaise, one part finely diced gherkin, a heaped spoon of capers chopped just enough to bleed, a squeeze of lemon, parsley, and black pepper. Do not rinse the capers — the brine is the seasoning. Rest it thirty minutes: it is watery at first and thickens as the pickle gives up its liquid into the emulsion.

In the game

Recipe(id: "tartare", inputs: [("diced_pickle", 1), ("capers", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("mayonnaise", 2)],
       fluid_outputs: [("tartare", 1)],
       machine: "mixer", time_ticks: 100),
Recipe(id: "bottled_tartare", fluid_inputs: [("tartare", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_tartare", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_tartare", inputs: [("bottled_tartare", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_tartare", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

A tap off the mayonnaise run, a belt of diced pickle from the crusher and a hopper of capers, all into a mixer (100t), then bottler, packer, dock. Mechanically this is bbq_mix's shipped shape exactly: two solids and one fluid at a mixer. What is new is where a solid comes from — diced pickle arrives off a line, not out of a hopper the player tops up.

Machines

None new. A sixth mixer recipe, a tenth bottler recipe, a tenth packer recipe.

Why it is fun

It is the first sauce whose art is a mechanic you can see. The flecks in the bottle are the pickle you routed, and a player who has just spent five minutes on a sub-assembly gets a bottle that visibly contains it. It is also the first sauce that can starve because a line you built is too slow rather than because a hopper ran dry — every solid input in the game today comes from a raw you buy, so "my crusher cannot keep up" is a sentence CONDIMENTAL has never made a player say. Beyond that it puts a fourth straw in the mayonnaise run, which is the pressure the mayo tier exists to create.

The money

81 per box: 66 of mayonnaise, 9 of diced pickle, 6 of capers. Contracts pay 225–250. Capers at 6 are the priciest raw in the book and they are only ever bought one at a time, which is deliberate — it is a small, steady drip of money leaving the factory rather than a bulk order, and it is the first ingredient a player will think about skipping. Gate: 170 lifetime deliveries, releasing cucumber, capers and the tartare contracts.

09 · Burger Sauce — DATA

IMG:card_09_burger_sauce

The point of it. The sauce that eats the tutorial. Burger sauce is mayonnaise, ketchup and pickle — and ketchup is the thing the player has been making since the first minute of the game. This is the tier where the starter line stops being the boring one.

In a kitchen

Four parts mayonnaise, two parts ketchup, one part finely diced gherkin, a teaspoon of the pickle brine, a pinch of paprika and a pinch of onion powder. That is genuinely the whole recipe — every burger sauce on every menu is a variation on those three things, and the ratio is what is being argued about, not the ingredients.

In the game

Recipe(id: "burger_sauce", inputs: [("diced_pickle", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("mayonnaise", 2), ("ketchup", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("burger_sauce", 1)],
       machine: "mixer", time_ticks: 100),
Recipe(id: "bottled_burger_sauce", fluid_inputs: [("burger_sauce", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_burger_sauce", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_burger_sauce", inputs: [("bottled_burger_sauce", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_burger_sauce", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Two taps — mayonnaise and ketchup — plus a belt of diced pickle, into a mixer (100t), then bottler, packer, dock. Both halves of that are shipped: BBQ's cook already draws two fluids, and BBQ's mixer already takes a solid alongside a fluid. Two fluids and a solid at one machine first appears earlier in this book, at 03, in mayo_base — so by the time burger sauce lands the shape is exercised and, unlike house reserve's three draws (07), it needs nothing new at all.

Machines

None new. A seventh mixer recipe, an eleventh bottler recipe, an eleventh packer recipe.

Why it is fun

Because it reaches all the way back to tile one. Ketchup has been a finished product for the whole game; here it becomes an ingredient, and the player has to tap the oldest, most settled pipe on their floor — the one they laid before they knew what they were doing — and steal from it. Some players will find their original ketchup line cannot spare the throughput and will have to go back and widen the thing they built first. That is the best kind of late-game content: it makes you re-read your own early work.

It is also the sauce that makes the tree look like a tree. Burger sauce and the house reserve are the only two nodes with three parents, and burger sauce is the one whose parents reach back to tile one — on the diagram the ketchup arrow runs the full width of the page to get to it.

The money

86 per box: 66 of mayonnaise, 11 of ketchup, 9 of diced pickle. Contracts pay 235–260, the best line in the book — and it should be, because it is the only sauce that competes with your own ketchup orders for supply. Every box of burger sauce is a box of ketchup you did not ship. Gate: 195 lifetime deliveries, releasing the burger-sauce contracts and nothing else: it introduces no raw of its own, and the cucumbers its pickles are made from arrived with tartare.

3 · Four things to build

IMG:plate_machines

Two sauces out of nine need new equipment — and the other seven need none at all, which is the tree paying for itself. This is all of it — three entries in data/machines.ron, four in data/shop.ron (a pipe is priced there without being a machine kind, exactly as plain pipe is today), and four voxel generators. The models above are concept art in the game's palette; they are not shipped assets.

Emulsifier — 1×2, manufacturing 1, 180

Threads oil into yolk under a high-shear head. Sits between the mixer (100) and the cooking vat (200) in price, and takes the same crew as a mixer. Visually the dosing line is the tell: a brass line running down from a small tank into the vessel, dripping. It is the only machine in the game where you can see the slow step, which matters because 150 ticks with no visible reason feels broken and 150 ticks with a dripping line feels deliberate. Cost to build: one data entry and one generator. No simulation work at all.

Chiller — 1×2, manufacturing 1, 150

Sets mayonnaise cold, and — the important part — while stock sits in a chiller's buffer, the spoil clock stops. It is the only white machine on the floor, with a frost window on the seen face, so "this part of my factory is cold" reads from the camera without a single UI element. Cost to build: one data entry, one generator, and the perishable axis (§6).

Fermenter — 2×2, manufacturing 2, 260

3 000 ticks, five times the longest process currently in the game (BBQ's cook, at 600). A 2×2 footprint and a two-person crew, so a bank of four is a real investment in floor and in payroll. Airlock bubbles on the lid are the motion frames — a machine that takes a hundred seconds needs to visibly be doing something the whole time — and a hazard band around the base says acid-rated, which is the visual link to lined pipe. It is the one new machine whose two recipes come from unrelated tiers — chilli mash for 05, cucumbers for the pickle line — so a bank bought for hot sauce is already paid for when tartare arrives. Cost to build: one data entry and one generator. No simulation work.

Lined pipe — 1×1, no crew, 24

Three times the price of plain pipe (8) and identical in every way except that a corrosive fluid does not eat it. Visually: the same pipe silhouette with an acid-yellow stripe, so a corrosive run is legible as a run rather than as a tooltip. Cost to build: one data entry, one generator, and the corrosion axis (§6).
