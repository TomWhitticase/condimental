6 · What this actually costs in code

Honest accounting. Seven of the nine sauces are data and art: recipes, ingredients, shop entries, contracts, unlocks, and one voxel generator per new thing. Two rules do not exist yet, and each is the price of admission for exactly one tier — and so for everything the tree hangs off it — which makes each a genuine go/no-go decision rather than a detail.

The perishable axis — buys mayonnaise (and everything downstream of it)

The rule. An ingredient may carry a shelf life in ticks. A stack of it sitting in a buffer — a hopper, a belt, a machine's input slot — ages; when its clock runs out it is destroyed and the loss is announced. A chiller's buffer does not run the clock.

What it touches. One optional field in data/ingredients.ron (shelf_life_ticks, absent meaning imperishable, so every existing entry is unchanged). A per-stack age in whatever holds a stack, which is the part to be careful about: ages must be fixed-point or integer ticks, they must be saved, and the save format changes. An event so the HUD can say what rotted and where, because silent destruction is indistinguishable from a bug.

What it is worth. It is the only rule in this document that changes how the whole factory is laid out rather than what it contains. Everything else here adds nodes to a graph; this one adds a cost to distance. That is a big lever for one field and one clock, and it is why mayonnaise is the tier worth arguing about.

The cheap version, if the answer is no. Make eggs imperishable and let mayonnaise be a straightforward two-new-machine tier. It loses the floor-plan lesson entirely and the chiller becomes decorative, but the sauce, the art and the economy all still work. Do not ship the chiller without the clock — a machine whose only job is to stop a clock that does not exist is a lie on the floor.

The corrosion axis — buys hot sauce

The rule. A fluid may be corrosive. A plain pipe carrying a corrosive fluid accumulates wear and eventually faults; lined pipe never does. Wear is visible on the pipe before it faults — this must not be a surprise.

What it touches. One optional field on a fluid, a per-pipe wear counter (saved), a fault state pipes do not currently have, and a tint or overlay on a worn pipe. It reuses whatever the andon stack-light work already gives machines for saying "something is wrong here".

What it is worth. Less than the perishable axis, honestly. Its real job is making the fermenter bank feel like specialist equipment rather than just another machine — you are building an acid corner of the factory, and it looks like one. That is a mood, not a mechanic.

The cheap version, if the answer is no. Drop corrosion, keep lined pipe as a pure requirement: a corrosive fluid simply cannot enter a plain pipe, refused at placement time the way an invalid connection already is. No wear, no clock, no save change — and it still forces the player to buy and route special pipe. This is the recommended fallback and it is close to free.

One thing the gates cannot say

Every Gate line in this document is written as a lifetime-delivery count, because that is the only trigger data/unlocks.ron has, and an Unlock names exactly two lists: ingredients and contracts. There is no field that holds a machine back. So the emulsifier, the chiller and the fermenter are all on sale from a new game, and what actually gates those tiers is that their raws cannot be bought and nobody is asking for the sauce — the same mechanism BBQ already ships with.

That is fine, and probably correct: a machine you can buy and cannot use is a worse lesson than a machine that appears when there is something to do with it. But if the shop list should grow as the game does, that is a third list on Unlock, a filter in the shop, and a Buy command that refuses a locked kind — small, but sim work, and it is not costed anywhere above.

7 · Decide this first

There is one shipped invariant that every sauce in this document runs into, and it has to be settled before any of them can land. From sim_core/src/sim/recipe.rs:

Recipes sharing a machine kind must take disjoint inputs, so that
what a machine is plumbed to *is* which recipe it runs and file
order never decides anything.

It is a good rule and it is load-bearing: it is the reason a machine needs no recipe dropdown. The proposals above break it in four places:

  • mixer: ketchup_mix and mustard_mix both consume vinegar.
  • crusher: garlic_paste and chilli_mash both consume salt.
  • mixer, again: chilli_mayo, tartare and burger_sauce all consume mayonnaise, and the last two also share diced_pickle.
  • mixer, a third time: bbq_mix and burger_sauce both consume ketchup — one shipped recipe and one proposed one, which is the clearest evidence that this is structural and not a slip in the proposals.

The first two are shared commodities — the kind of ingredient that ought to be shared. The last two are worse and more interesting: by sauce 09 the mixer hosts seven recipes drawn from an overlapping pool of inputs, most of them things the factory makes itself, which is what a sauce tree inevitably produces. This will happen again with sugar and with oil. There are three ways out.

A — Dodge it in data

Give each sauce its own raw: cider_vinegar for mustard, salt folded into the fermenter recipe instead of the crusher. Costs no code at all. Costs the player a second vinegar tank for no reason they can see, and multiplies ingredient ids forever — by sauce twelve there are four vinegars. Not recommended, but it is the escape hatch if the release is close.

B — Relax the invariant (recommended)

Replace "disjoint inputs" with: of any two recipes on one machine kind, each must require at least one input id the other does not — neither's input set may be a subset of the other's. A machine then runs the one recipe all of whose inputs it is actually plumbed to. All three violations above resolve cleanly: a mixer fed tomato paste, sugar and vinegar can only be making ketchup mix; a crusher fed garlic and salt can only be making garlic paste; a mixer fed mayonnaise, ketchup and diced pickle can only be making burger sauce.

Ambiguity is not impossible under this rule, and pretending otherwise is the trap. The subset rule stops one recipe from swallowing another, but it does nothing about a machine plumbed to the union of two — and the union satisfies both. Tartare needs mayonnaise, diced pickle and capers; burger sauce needs mayonnaise, ketchup and diced pickle; neither is a subset of the other, and a mixer reached by all four of those inputs qualifies for both. That is not a hypothetical, it is sauces 08 and 09 sitting next to each other.

So the rule needs a stated answer for it, and "whichever is first in the file" is exactly the answer the shipped invariant exists to forbid. The answer should be: a machine that qualifies for two recipes runs neither. It stops, it lights its andon amber, and its panel says which two it is torn between and asks the player to pick — the recipe dropdown of option C, but appearing only in the one case where the factory genuinely has not said what it wants, and disappearing again when a pipe is moved. Placement stays the language; the dropdown becomes the error message.

The property the original test protects — file order never decides anything — survives intact, which is the part that actually matters.

What it costs: rewrite the invariant test to the new rule, change recipe selection to "the unique fully-satisfied recipe, or none", add the ambiguous state and its andon light, and — this is the part not to skip — have the machine panel name the recipe it has picked. The moment two recipes can live on one kind, a player needs to be told which one this machine is running, and that is a real UI surface, not a label.

C — Let the player pick

A recipe dropdown on the machine panel. This is what every other factory game does, and it is what the shipped invariant exists specifically to avoid — it turns placement from a spatial puzzle into a configuration chore. Not recommended, and mentioned only so the decision is made on purpose rather than by default.

Recommendation: B, before sauce 01. It is a small change made while there are two sauces, or a large one made while there are eleven.

8 · Deliberately not proposed

  • Ageing. Real mustard rests three days and real hot sauce ferments for weeks, and neither is in the game as a resting mechanic. aged_ketchup and the ageing rack were removed with the note "ageing is not a mechanic this game wants", and nothing here reopens that. The fermenter is not an ageing rack: it is an ordinary machine with a long time_ticks, it occupies the floor while it runs, and its output goes straight down a pipe. House reserve is a prestige tier gated by layout, not by waiting.
  • A spot market. Every sauce here sells through contracts. Nothing in this document needs a place to dump surplus, and adding one would take the teeth out of the contention that most of these sauces are built to create.
  • Spice heat as a stat. Tempting, and wrong: a Scoville number would be a second economy running alongside the first, and none of the fun described above depends on it.
  • Quality tiers. Same argument. A sauce is a sauce; the interesting variable is whether you can make enough of it in time.
  • Per-sauce staff roles. The existing manufacturing crew covers all three new machines, and lined pipe takes no crew at all. A "fermenter tech" role would be a payroll line with no decision attached to it.
