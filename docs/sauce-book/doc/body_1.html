The Sauce Book

Nine sauces after BBQ — cooking recipes, production lines, machines, art. CONDIMENTAL, pre-alpha, 25 September 2026.

Every image and its source (card and plate renders, the voxel scripts, this doc as HTML) is archived in the repo on branch docs/sauce-book-assets: https://github.com/TomWhitticase/condimental/tree/docs/sauce-book-assets/docs/sauce-book

IMG:plate_cover

Source: art/plate_cover.jpg

BBQ proved the shape: a second sauce does not need a second factory, it needs a tap — a pipe off a fluid the factory already makes, taken before the bottler. Everything below is built on that move. Seven of the nine sauces here are a data-file edit: recipes, ingredients, shop prices, contracts, and one voxel generator each. Two buy a whole new axis of play, and those two are where the interesting design risk lives.

How to read this

  • DATA — new entries in data/*.ron plus voxel generators. No Rust, no GDScript.
  • NEW MACHINE — the above, plus a machine kind and its model. Still no sim work.
  • NEW AXIS — the above, plus a rule the simulation does not have yet. Two sauces do this; both are called out with what exactly it costs.

Every kitchen recipe below is a real one, scaled and simplified until it maps onto a stage of the line. Every game recipe is written in the shape data/recipes.ron already uses, so it can be pasted in and argued with. Prices follow the shipped rule — a box's ingredients cost about a third of what a contract pays for it. Machine art is concept art: drawn in the game's real palette (assets/voxels/palette.ron), rendered with a sketch tool, not the shipped vengi pipeline. Item art for shipped things is the shipped icon.

1 · The sauce tree

IMG:plate_tree

Source: art/plate_tree.jpg

Every arrow is a sauce used as an ingredient in another sauce. This is the argument for the whole document: a tree rather than a list means late capital pays off across every branch at once. Buying the emulsifier does not unlock mayonnaise, it unlocks mayonnaise and aioli and chilli mayo and tartare and burger sauce — five sellable sauces off one purchase. Three of the nine sauces below need no new machine and no new raw at all once their parents exist — 06, 07 and 09 are three recipe entries apiece (a blend, a bottle and a box) — and four of the remaining six need a raw and nothing more.

Four things on the diagram have no sauce above them — ketchup, mustard, hot sauce and the pickle branch each start at a raw you buy. Two of those are hubs, and they are deliberately different. Ketchup is the tutorial root, and by sauce 09 it is a supplier as much as a product: BBQ taps it, the house reserve takes it at one remove, and burger sauce taps it again. Mustard is the new root, and it is the busy one: it feeds honey mustard, it is an input to mayonnaise (mustard is the emulsifier in real mayonnaise, not a flavouring), and it lands in the house reserve. Put mustard early and everything downstream gets cheaper to reach.

One node on that tree is not a sauce. Pickles (the blue node) are feedstock — a thing the factory makes and never sells, the first of its kind in the game. Two sauces eat them and nobody ships them, which makes the pickle line the first bit of your factory that exists purely to serve another bit of your factory. That is a Factorio feeling the game does not have yet.

2 · The nine sauces

01 · Wholegrain Mustard — DATA

IMG:card_01_mustard

Source: art/card_01_mustard.jpg

The point of it. The first sauce that is not ketchup-shaped, and the one that teaches fluid contention without a single new mechanic. It reuses every machine kind in the game and still forces a rebuild, because it drinks four vinegar a batch where ketchup drinks two. One tank cannot feed both lines. The splitter you have never needed becomes the answer.

In a kitchen

100 g yellow mustard seed and 40 g brown, soaked 24 hours in 150 ml cider vinegar and 50 ml water. Add 1 tsp ground turmeric, 1 tsp salt, 1 tbsp honey. Pulse-grind half the seed and leave the rest whole — that split is what makes it wholegrain. Warm gently to 60 °C for twenty minutes to knock back the raw bite, then rest two days before jarring; mustard is unpleasantly harsh on day one and rounds out on day three. (The rest is the one thing the game will not model — see §8.)

In the game

Recipe(id: "mustard_grits", inputs: [("mustard_seed", 3)],
       outputs: [("mustard_grits", 1)], machine: "crusher", time_ticks: 70),
Recipe(id: "mustard_mix", inputs: [("mustard_grits", 1), ("turmeric", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("vinegar", 4)], fluid_outputs: [("mustard_mix", 1)],
       machine: "mixer", time_ticks: 90),
Recipe(id: "mustard", fluid_inputs: [("mustard_mix", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("mustard", 1)], machine: "cooking_vat", time_ticks: 180),
Recipe(id: "bottled_mustard", fluid_inputs: [("mustard", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_mustard", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_mustard", inputs: [("bottled_mustard", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_mustard", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Hopper of mustard seed → crusher (70t, mills the seed to grits) → belt → mixer (90t, grits plus a hopper of turmeric, drinking vinegar from a tank) → pipe → cooking vat (180t, a warm steep, not a reduction — shorter than ketchup's 240) → pipe → bottler → belt → packer → dock. Structurally it is the ketchup chain with different ids, which is the point: a player who can build ketchup can build this on muscle memory, and then discovers the vinegar bill.

Machines

None new. A second crusher recipe, and a third on the mixer, the cooking vat, the bottler and the packer — every kind already on your floor. This is where the recipe-ambiguity problem bites (§7): ketchup_mix and mustard_mix both run on the mixer and both consume vinegar, which the shipped invariant test forbids. That decision has to be made before this sauce can ship.

Why it is fun

Because the trap is legible and the fix is satisfying. You build the mustard line, both lines run for ninety seconds, and then both starve — not one. The vinegar tank is the shared thing and nothing on screen said so. The fix is a second tank, or a splitter with a real decision about which side gets priority. It is the first time the factory pushes back on a resource rather than on geometry, and it costs no new code to teach.

The money

18 per box in ingredients (seed 2×3, turmeric 4, vinegar 2×4). Ketchup is 11, BBQ is 23. Contracts pay 48–56 — deliberately thin, because the sauce's job is to make you fix your plumbing, not to make you rich. Gate: 25 lifetime deliveries, releasing mustard_seed, turmeric and the mustard contracts.

02 · Honey Mustard — DATA

IMG:card_02_honey_mustard

Source: art/card_02_honey_mustard.jpg

The point of it. Three lines in recipes.ron and one new raw — and the first sauce in the game that is never cooked. It taps the mustard run before the bottler and goes straight to a mixer and out. A derived sauce that skips the slowest machine is free throughput, and that is a lesson worth teaching exactly once.

In a kitchen

Three parts finished wholegrain mustard, two parts honey, one part cider vinegar, a pinch of salt. Whisk cold. Do not heat it: honey's aromatics are volatile and heat drives them off, leaving sugar — which is why this is a blend and not a cook, in the kitchen and in the game.

In the game

Recipe(id: "honey_mustard", fluid_inputs: [("mustard", 1), ("honey", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("honey_mustard", 1)], machine: "mixer", time_ticks: 110),
Recipe(id: "bottled_honey_mustard", fluid_inputs: [("honey_mustard", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_honey_mustard", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_honey_mustard", inputs: [("bottled_honey_mustard", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_honey_mustard", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

A pipe off the mustard vat's run — the same tap BBQ takes off ketchup — into a mixer (110t, cold blend) fed by a tank of honey. Pipe to a bottler, belt to a packer, belt to the dock. Three machines and a dock. It is the shortest sellable line in the game — only chilli mayo (06) ever matches it — and it should stay that way.

Machines

None new. One more mixer recipe, one more bottler recipe, one more packer recipe.

Why it is fun

It is the reward beat. Mustard just cost you a rebuild; honey mustard is three machines bolted to the side of it that sells for more per box than mustard does. It also quietly teaches the tap as a tactic rather than a curiosity: you now have two sauces competing for one mustard run, and the question "do I bottle it or blend it?" has a board-driven answer that changes every few minutes.

The money

22 per box, of which 18 is mustard you made yourself — the first sauce whose bill is mostly your own labour. Contracts pay 58–64. Gate: 35 lifetime deliveries, releasing honey and the honey-mustard contracts.
