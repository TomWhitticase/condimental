03 · Mayonnaise — NEW MACHINE + NEW AXIS

IMG:card_03_mayonnaise

The point of it. The cold-chain tier. Two new machines, and the first ingredient in the game that can go off. Mayonnaise is where the factory stops being a graph of throughputs and starts being a floor plan with a clock on it.

In a kitchen

Two egg yolks, 1 tsp mustard, 1 tbsp vinegar or lemon juice, a pinch of salt. Whisk, then add 250 ml neutral oil a drop at a time at first, in a thin stream once it takes. Everything at room temperature or the emulsion splits; the finished jar goes straight to the fridge. The mustard is not flavouring — it is the emulsifier, which is why mustard is an input in the game recipe and why mustard has to exist before mayonnaise can.

In the game

Recipe(id: "mayo_base", inputs: [("egg", 2)],
       fluid_inputs: [("oil", 3), ("mustard", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("mayo_base", 1)],
       machine: "emulsifier", time_ticks: 150),
Recipe(id: "mayonnaise", fluid_inputs: [("mayo_base", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("mayonnaise", 1)],
       machine: "chiller", time_ticks: 90),
Recipe(id: "bottled_mayonnaise", fluid_inputs: [("mayonnaise", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_mayonnaise", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_mayonnaise", inputs: [("bottled_mayonnaise", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_mayonnaise", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

A hopper of eggs, a tank of oil and a tap off the mustard run all meet at the emulsifier (150t — slow, and visibly slow: the dosing arm is the animation). Pipe to the chiller (90t), pipe to the bottler, belt, packer, dock. Two fluid draws at one machine is a move BBQ's cook already ships — but taking them alongside a solid is not, so mayo_base is the first recipe in the game to do both at once. That is a combination of two exercised paths rather than a new capability, and it is where the shape gets its first test; sauces 08 and 09 inherit it. What is new for the player is that the feed belt from the egg hopper is now a liability.

Machines

Emulsifier (new, 1×2, manufacturing 1, 180) and Chiller (new, 1×2, manufacturing 1, 150). Full specs in §3. The chiller is the only white machine on the floor, so cold reads at a glance across the whole factory.

Why it is fun

Because for the first time the thing beating you is your own floor plan. Eggs spoil: a spoil clock runs on every buffer that is not a chiller, and a long, pretty, sprawling feed belt now kills stock outright before it arrives. Compact wins; sprawl rots. It inverts the habit the first four hours taught — long belts are cheap, so make them long — and it does it with one number on one ingredient. The chiller then becomes a verb: players will start parking chillers as buffers, which is exactly the emergent use worth designing for.

The money

33 per box (egg 3×2, oil 3×3, plus 18 of mustard). Contracts pay 92–104 — the first real jump in the game, and it should feel like one. Gate: 45 lifetime deliveries, releasing egg, oil and the mayonnaise contracts. The emulsifier and the chiller are simply on sale from a new game: data/unlocks.ron has no field that holds a machine back, and §6 says what adding one would cost.

04 · Garlic Aioli — DATA

IMG:card_04_aioli

The point of it. A variant, not a tier: one crusher recipe and one more emulsifier recipe on machines you already own. Aioli steals mayonnaise exactly the way BBQ steals ketchup — same run, same contention — and what it adds is a contract board that can ask for both.

In a kitchen

Four garlic cloves and half a teaspoon of coarse salt pounded to a smooth paste in a mortar — the salt is the abrasive, so do not skip it — then folded into 250 ml of mayonnaise with lemon to taste. Traditional Catalan allioli is garlic and oil alone, emulsified by hand, and is a nightmare; every restaurant kitchen on earth cheats with mayonnaise, so the game cheats too.

In the game

Recipe(id: "garlic_paste", inputs: [("garlic", 2), ("salt", 1)],
       outputs: [("garlic_paste", 1)], machine: "crusher", time_ticks: 80),
Recipe(id: "aioli", inputs: [("garlic_paste", 1)],
       fluid_inputs: [("mayonnaise", 1)], fluid_outputs: [("aioli", 1)],
       machine: "emulsifier", time_ticks: 90),
Recipe(id: "bottled_aioli", fluid_inputs: [("aioli", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_aioli", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_aioli", inputs: [("bottled_aioli", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_aioli", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Hoppers of garlic and salt → crusher (80t) → belt → a second emulsifier (90t) tapping the mayonnaise run → bottler → packer → dock. Note the second emulsifier: the first one is saturated making mayo base at 150t, so aioli is also the moment a player learns to buy a duplicate of a machine rather than a new kind.

Machines

None new. A third crusher recipe and a second emulsifier recipe. Under §7's recommended rule these are unambiguous — mayo_base needs eggs and aioli needs garlic paste, so what the machine is plumbed to still decides what it runs.

Why it is fun

Because it makes the board harder without making the factory harder. "40 mayonnaise and 20 aioli" is a genuinely nastier order than "60 of either" off one mayo run, and the player has to decide whether to split the run, stagger the contracts, or buy a third emulsifier. That is a scheduling problem, and the game has not posed one yet.

The money

38 per box, 33 of it mayonnaise. Contracts pay 104–118. Gate: 60 lifetime deliveries, releasing garlic, salt and the aioli contracts.

05 · Hot Sauce — NEW MACHINE + NEW AXIS

IMG:card_05_hot_sauce

The point of it. The acid tier. A 3 000-tick ferment and — for the first time — a factory that has to grow sideways.

There is one hot sauce in this game and it is fermented. Hot sauce is a category in the real world with two production routes — quick, where vinegar supplies the acid, and fermented, where lactic acid bacteria make it over weeks — and the game only wants one of them. The ferment is the route worth simulating, because a process that takes 3 000 ticks is a design problem and a process that takes 120 is a formality. So the sauce is called Hot Sauce, its fluid id is hot_sauce, and the fermenter in the middle of its line is simply how it is made. Every later reference — chilli mayo's second tap, house reserve's third draw — is this one fluid.

A second, unfermented "quick hot sauce" is a real option and is deliberately not taken: a cheap alternative standing next to the fermenter would undercut the one thing the fermenter exists to be, which is slow. It belongs on the §5 list later, if ever, once players have built a fermenter bank and have opinions about it.

In a kitchen

500 g red chillies, stems off, blended coarse with 15 g salt — 3 % by weight, which is the whole recipe's only real number. Pack into a jar under an airlock, leave at 20 °C for one to three weeks until it stops bubbling and smells sour rather than sweet. Blend with 100 ml vinegar, strain, and pasteurise to 80 °C so it stops changing in the bottle. The ferment is what separates a hot sauce from hot vinegar, and it is slow: this is genuinely the longest process in this document.

In the game

Recipe(id: "chilli_mash", inputs: [("chilli", 3), ("salt", 1)],
       outputs: [("chilli_mash", 1)], machine: "crusher", time_ticks: 80),
Recipe(id: "fermented_mash", inputs: [("chilli_mash", 1)],
       fluid_outputs: [("fermented_mash", 1)],
       machine: "fermenter", time_ticks: 3000),
Recipe(id: "hot_sauce", fluid_inputs: [("fermented_mash", 1), ("vinegar", 2)],
       fluid_outputs: [("hot_sauce", 1)],
       machine: "cooking_vat", time_ticks: 120),
Recipe(id: "bottled_hot_sauce", fluid_inputs: [("hot_sauce", 1)],
       outputs: [("bottled_hot_sauce", 1)], machine: "bottler", time_ticks: 60),
Recipe(id: "boxed_hot_sauce", inputs: [("bottled_hot_sauce", 1)],
       outputs: [("boxed_hot_sauce", 1)], machine: "packer", time_ticks: 40),

The line

Hoppers of chilli and salt → crusher (80t) → belt → fermenter (3 000t, 2×2) → lined pipe → cooking vat (120t, pasteurise, drinking vinegar) → pipe → bottler → packer → dock. Only the first run is corrosive. The raw mash is the live, acid-making thing and pasteurising in the vat is exactly what stops it — so finished hot sauce travels in ordinary pipe, and so does every later tap off it (06, 07). That matters: if the finished fluid were corrosive too, chilli mayo would stop being free.

Machines

Fermenter (new, 2×2, manufacturing 2, 260) and lined pipe (new, 1×1, no crew, 24 — three times plain pipe). Full specs in §3.

Why it is fun

One fermenter makes a unit every hundred seconds. Nothing you build downstream fixes that — a faster vat, a second bottler, more staff, none of it matters. The only answer is a bank of fermenters, so for the first time the factory grows sideways instead of longer, and a corner of the floor becomes a farm of slow identical machines quietly bubbling. That is a Factorio feeling the game does not have yet, and it is worth a machine kind to get.

The corrosion rule is the second half: it means you cannot simply reuse the pipe you have lying around. A player who runs plain pipe on fermented mash will watch it fault mid-shift, and the fix is obvious, cheap and memorable — which is what a good punishment looks like.

The money

11 per box (chilli 2×3, salt 1, vinegar 2×2) — the cheapest bill in the game — and contracts pay 66–74. It is deliberately the best margin per box and the worst margin per minute: you can only make so much of it, so you are buying patience, not profit. Gate: 75 lifetime deliveries, releasing chilli and the hot-sauce contracts. As with mayonnaise, the fermenter and the lined pipe are on sale from the start — what gates the tier is the chilli, not the kit.
